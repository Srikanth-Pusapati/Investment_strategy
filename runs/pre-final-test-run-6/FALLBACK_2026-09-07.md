### 6.1 Rule 4 will start counting on **Sep 9** and currently reads **beta 0.06**

This is the one thing that needs you before verdict day.

```
sessions=3  alpha/day=+0.802%  t(alpha)=2.65  beta=0.06  r2=0.02

```

Rule 4 requires realized OLS beta in **0.8–1.2** and is judged "when the window
has ≥ 5 sessions". The checker counts *sessions as return-pairs*, so it becomes a
**counted** rule at the **Sep 9 close** (6 close rows → 5 pairs). Two sessions away.

The ex-ante controller is doing its job perfectly — `book_beta_spy` has sat at
1.04–1.11 all window and the hedge held inside the ±0.15 band on all 8 cycles
Sep 4. The problem is that **rule 4 grades *realized* beta, and the strategy
working is what destroys it.** The three OLS pairs are:

| | book | SPY |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 2 | +0.468% | +0.444% |
| Sep 3 | +1.076% | +1.047% |
| Sep 4 | **+0.931%** | **−0.385%** |

Sep 2 and Sep 3 track SPY nearly 1:1. Sep 4 — the book up 0.93% while SPY fell —
single-handedly flattens the slope to 0.06 and pushes alpha to +0.80%/day. **Every
additional down-day on which the book makes money drives rule 4 further toward
NO-GO.** With ~8 pairs by Sep 11, one idiosyncratic day dominates the estimate;
r² is 0.02, i.e. the beta is statistically meaningless, but the rule reads it anyway.

A related quirk: the checker takes returns *between consecutive in-window closes*,
so **Sep 1 is silently dropped** from rules 3 and 4 (no in-window predecessor).
That discards the window's best relative day — book +0.094% against SPY −0.687%,
a +0.78 pp edge. Rules 3/4 are measuring 3 of 4 sessions.

This is a **contract question, not a knob** — rule 4 was pre-registered assuming
realized ≈ ex-ante. I did not touch it. Your options, all requiring a
pre-registered amendment before Sep 9: grade rule 4 on the stamped ex-ante
`book_beta_spy` instead of OLS; keep OLS but require the ≥20-session sample that
rule 3 already demands of its t-stat; or accept the risk and let it fail.
